\subsection{Material dependence of resistance R\label{Materialabhängigkeit des Widerstandes}}
\Aufgabe{
	 A copper wire has the following properties:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Length: \( l = 5\,mathrm{m} \)
  \item Cross-sectional area: \( A = \SI{1}{\milli\meter\squared} \)
  \item Specific resistance of copper: \( \rho = \SI{1.68e-8}{\ohm\meter} \)
\end{itemize}
Calculate \( R \) of the wire.
}

\subsection{Electrical resistance \label{Elektrischer Widerstand}}

\Aufgabe{

A spherical hollow structure consists of two ideally conductive shells – an inner one with radius \( a \) and an outer one with radius \( b \).

The space between them is completely filled with a homogeneous, conductive material that has electrical conductivity \( \kappa \) (in \si{\siemens\per\metre}).

A voltage
\[
U = \varphi_1 - \varphi_2
\]
is applied between the conductive shells by a voltage source. 

The current can spread unhindered due to the complete filling and ideal contacts.

\medskip

Calculate the electrical resistance \( R \) between the two spherical shells as a function of \( a \), \( b \) and \( \kappa \).

}

\subsection{Electrical resistance of a coated wire\label{Elektrischer Widerstand eines beschichteten Drahts}}

\Aufgabe{

A round aluminium wire has a radius of \( a = \SI{0.5}{\milli\meter} \).  
It is coated with a thin layer of gold. \( b = \SI{0.1}{\milli\meter} \) Thickness coated. 

The following values apply to the specific electrical conductivity and temperature coefficient of the two metals:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Aluminium:} 
  \[
  \kappa_\text{Alu} = \SI{35}{\meter \per (\ohm \milli\meter\squared)}, \quad \alpha_\text{Alu} = 3{,}5 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\si{\per\kelvin}
  \]
  
  \item \textbf{Gold:} 
  \[
  \kappa_\text{Gold} = \SI{44}{\meter \per (\ohm \milli\meter\squared)}, \quad \alpha_\text{Gold} = 3{,}6 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\si{\per\kelvin}
  \]
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Given:}  
Wire Length: \( l = \SI{1.5}{\meter} \)  
Temperature: \( T = \SI{25}{\celsius} \) (Initial temperature)

\begin{itemize}
  \item[a)] Calculate the electrical DC resistance of the aluminium core and gold coating at \( T = \SI{25}{\celsius} \).
  \item[b)] How does the respective direct current resistance change when the temperature rises to \( T = 90 \celsius \)?
\end{itemize}


}

\subsection{Inductance and coil\label{Induktivität und Spüle}}

\Aufgabe{

\textbf{Part 1: Energy in the coil}

Given is a coil with the inductance
\[
L = \SI{2}{\henry}
\]
and a current
\[
I = \SI{3}{\ampere}.
\]

\begin{itemize}
    \item[a)] Calculate the energy stored in the magnetic field of the coil.
    \item[b)] How does the energy change when the current is reduced to \( I = \SI{0.5}{\ampere} \)?
\end{itemize}

\vspace{1em}

\textbf{Part 2: Inductance of a cylindrical coil}

A cylindrical coil has
\[
N = 500 \text{ Wicklungen}, \quad l = \SI{20}{\centi\meter}, \quad A = \SI{5}{\centi\meter\squared}
\]
The inside of the coil is filled with air.

Calculate the inductance \( L \) of the coil.

}

\subsection{Inductance, magnetic flux density and magnetic field energy\label{Induktivität, Magnetische Flussdichte und Magnetfeldenergie}}

\Aufgabe{

An air-filled, long cylindrical coil conductor has the following properties:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Number of turns: \( N = 500 \)
  \item Length of the coil: \( l = \SI{0.5}{\meter} \)
  \item Cross-sectional area: \( A = 4 \cdot 10^{-4} \, \si{\meter\squared} \)
  \item Current: \( I = \SI{2.5}{\ampere} \)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Given:}  
Magnetic field constant: \( \mu_0 = 4\pi \cdot 10^{-7} \, \si{\henry\per\meter} \)

\begin{itemize}
  \item[a)] Calculate the magnetic flux density \( \vec{B} \) inside the coil.
  \item[b)] Determine the inductance \( L \) of the coil.
  \item[c)] Calculate the energy \( E_m \) stored in the magnetic field.
\end{itemize}

}
