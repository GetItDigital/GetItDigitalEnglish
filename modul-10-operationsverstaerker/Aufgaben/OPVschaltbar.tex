\Loesung{
    \begin{itemize}
        \item[a)] Non-inverting amplifier  
        The gain can be changed using two switches (S1 and S2), resulting in four possible combinations:

        \textbf{Case 1 (S1 closed, S2 open):}  
        In this state, resistors $R_\mathrm{n1}$ and $R_\mathrm{n2}$ form a series connection. The total gain is therefore:  
        \[
        V = 1 + \frac{R_\mathrm{f}}{R_\mathrm{n1} + R_\mathrm{n2}}
        \]

        \textbf{Case 2 (S1 open, S2 closed):}  
        Here, resistor $R_\mathrm{f}$ and $R_\mathrm{n1}$ are in series. This series combination, together with resistor $R_\mathrm{n2}$, forms the feedback loop. The gain is therefore:  
        \[
        V = 1 + \frac{R_\mathrm{f} + R_\mathrm{n1}}{R_\mathrm{n2}}
        \]

        \textbf{Case 3 (S1 and S2 closed):}  
        With both switches closed, resistor $R_\mathrm{n1}$ is shorted and thus not considered. The gain is:  
        \[
        V = 1 + \frac{R_\mathrm{f}}{R_\mathrm{n2}}
        \]

        \textbf{Case 4 (S1 and S2 open):}  
        If both switches are open, the feedback path is missing completely, so the gain is theoretically infinite:  
        \[
        V = \infty
        \]

        \item[b)] Inverting amplifier  
        \textbf{Case 1 (S1 open):}  
        If $R_\mathrm{f1}$ and $R_\mathrm{f2}$ are in series, this results in more feedback and thus higher gain. The gain is calculated as:  
        \[
        V = -\frac{R_\mathrm{f1} + R_\mathrm{f2}}{R_\mathrm{n}}
        \]

        \textbf{Case 2 (S1 closed, S2 open):}  
        In this state, resistor $R_\mathrm{f2}$ is shorted by the closed switch and not considered. The gain is reduced to:  
        \[
        V = -\frac{R_\mathrm{f1}}{R_\mathrm{n}}
        \]
    \end{itemize}
