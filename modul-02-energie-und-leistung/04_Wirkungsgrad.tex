	\section{The efficiency}
	
\s{
Efficiency describes the ratio of output power (useful power) 
to input power (energy supplied) in a system and is a measure of the efficiency with which 
a system converts energy. Using the example of an electric motor, Figure \ref{fig:Wirkungsgrad} shows how its power balance is composed.
}

\s{
	The electric motor is supplied with electrical energy at the input, which it absorbs as input power $P_\mathrm{zu}$.
	The desired output power $P_\mathrm{ab}$ in this case is of a rotary nature. The waste heat generated by the motor, as well as the noise and friction it causes, together result in the power loss $P_\mathrm{v}$. 
}


\subsection{Calculation of efficiency}

\s{
Efficiency is typically expressed as a percentage. An efficiency of 100%
means that all of the energy supplied is effectively converted into useful energy.
However, this is not achievable in practice due to losses, usually in the form of heat, friction or sound.
Mathematically, efficiency $\eta$ (eta) is defined as follows:

\begin{equation}
	\eta = \frac{P_\mathrm{{ab}}}{P_\mathrm{{zu}}} \cdot 100 \%
\end{equation}
}

	
\b{
\begin{equation*}

\end{equation*}
}

\subsection{Loss mechanisms of a light source}

A prime example of the relevance of efficiency is the classic incandescent light bulb. It produces light by
passing electricity through a wire, which heats up so much that it begins to glow.
Less than 5 per cent of the energy used is converted into light.
More than 95\% of the energy is converted into heat. Due to their poor efficiency, incandescent light bulbs have been gradually banned in the EU since 2009. 
Figure \ref{fig:Energieumwandlung Glühlampe und Energiesparlampe} shows a classic incandescent light bulb on the left and an energy-saving light bulb on the right.

\s{
	High efficiency is desirable in many applications, as it means that less
	energy is converted into forms of energy that are generally undesirable, and instead remains available to the system as useful energy. 
	In practice, however, many factors such as material properties,
	design and operating conditions are crucial for maximising efficiency.
	Efficiency plays a crucial role in environmental and energy technology, as it is directly
	related to energy consumption and environmental impact. More efficient systems
	can help to reduce energy demand and greenhouse gas emissions.

\begin{expl}{Calculation of efficiency}{}

%Hier kommt ein Beispiel zur Berechnung des Wirkungsgrades rein.

A heat engine extracts energy of \( Q_\text{zu} = 5000 \,\mathrm{J} \) from a heat source and performs mechanical work of \( W = 1200 \,\mathrm{J} \) in the process..

		\vspace{0.3cm}
		\textbf{Wanted:} Efficiency \( \eta \)

		\vspace{0.2cm}
		\textbf{Formula:}
		\[
			\eta = \frac{\text{Nutzarbeit}}{\text{Zugeführte Wärmeenergie}} \cdot 100\% = \frac{W}{Q_\text{zu}} \cdot 100\%
		\]

		\vspace{0.2cm}
		\textbf{Insert:}
		\[
			\eta = \frac{1200\,\text{J}}{5000\,\text{J}} \cdot 100\% = 24\%
		\]

		\vspace{0.2cm}
		The efficiency is \( \eta = 24\% \).

\end{expl}
