\subsection{Voltage divider with operational amplifier as voltage follower\label{Aufg27}}
% Alt: Praktikumsaufgabe 13
\Aufgabe{
        Draw a new circuit diagram consisting of a loaded voltage divider supplemented by an operational amplifier as an impedance converter.  
        The component values are given as follows:
        
        \begin{itemize}
            \item Resistances: $R_1 = R_2 = 10\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$
            \item Load resistance: $R_\mathrm{L} = 2{,}2\,\mathrm{k\Omega}$
            \item Supply voltage: $\mathrm{U_{CC} = +15\,V}$
        \end{itemize}
        
        The operational amplifier is connected as an impedance converter (voltage follower).  
        Its output feeds the load resistor. $R_\mathrm{L}$.
        
        \begin{itemize}
            \item[a)] Calculate the output voltage $U_\mathrm{C}$ with and without the load resistor $\mathrm{R_L}$.  
            \item[b)] Compare the results with those from Exercise 13.  
            \item[c)] Explain why the voltage $U_\mathrm{C}$ does not change due to the load resistor in this circuit.
        \end{itemize}   
}

    \begin{itemize}
        \item[a)] Calculation of the output voltage $U_\mathrm{C}$ with and without load resistor \\  
        Since the operational amplifier is connected as a voltage follower (impedance buffer), the output voltage $U_\mathrm{A}$ exactly corresponds to the voltage across the voltage divider:
    \[
    U_\mathrm{A} = U_\mathrm{C} = 7{,}5\,\mathrm{V}
    \]
    Since the operational amplifier has a very high input resistance, the voltage is not affected by the load resistance.
    Thus, the output voltage remains constant at 7.5 V, regardless of whether $R_\mathrm{L}$ is connected or not.

    \item[b)] Compare with task 13. \\
    In contrast to the first circuit, the voltage does not drop when the load resistor $R_\mathrm{L}$ is connected.
    The operational amplifier decouples the voltage divider from the load, so that the voltage divider always maintains its original voltage.

    \item[c)] Explanation of voltage stability through the operational amplifier \\
    As an impedance converter, the operational amplifier has a high input resistance and a low output resistance.  
    As a result, the load resistance is no longer directly connected to the voltage divider, but receives its current from the OPV.  
    This means that the output voltage remains stable, regardless of the load.
    \end{itemize}
